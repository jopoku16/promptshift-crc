\subsection{Real model outputs}\label{sec:res-real}

We ran three open models on CPU and recorded their outputs (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}): Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for MMLU and TruthfulQA, GPT-2 for RealToxicityPrompts, and Qwen2.5-0.5B for the replication. The risk is $1-p(\text{correct})$ for the two question-answering tasks and the toxicity probability of a continuation for the third. The tasks differ in one way that matters for this paper: how much the \emph{upper tail} of the risk moves between regimes, because the threshold $q_t$ targets the $0.9$ quantile.

\paragraph{MMLU and TruthfulQA: the upper tail does not move.}
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct answers \MmluAcc{} of the \MmluN{} MMLU questions correctly. Across the four broad categories the median risk moves a great deal (from \MmluMedLo{} to \MmluMedHi{}) but the $0.9$ quantile does not (\MmluQnineLo{} to \MmluQnineHi{}). A threshold at the $0.9$ quantile is therefore close to $1$ in every regime, and the methods tie (Table~\ref{tab:mmlu_catblock}). In the category-block orderings static calibration already has post-shift violation \MmluCatStaticPost{}, against \MmluCatPSPost{} for PromptShift-CRC and \MmluCatMatchedPost{} for matched Adaptive CRC; the mean thresholds of the online methods lie within $0.015$ of one another; and the intervention rate is $0.999$ or higher for every method, so the decision rule almost never lets the model answer. PromptShift-CRC minus matched Adaptive CRC is \MmluCatPSvsMatchedPost{} (SE \MmluCatPSvsMatchedPostSE{}) in post-shift violation.

The frontier gaps are \MmluCatFrontierPS{} (SE \MmluCatFrontierPSSE{}) for PromptShift-CRC and \MmluCatFrontierDtACI{} (SE \MmluCatFrontierDtACISE{}) for DtACI in the category-block orderings, and \MmluSubjFrontierPS{} and \MmluSubjFrontierDtACI{} in the subject-block orderings. In \MmluCatFrontierPSOutN{} of the \MmluCatFrontierPSAllN{} category-block orderings the mean threshold of PromptShift-CRC lies outside the range of the window sweep, where the frontier is clipped to its nearest end; over the \MmluCatFrontierPSInN{} orderings inside the range the PromptShift-CRC gap is \MmluCatFrontierPSIn{} (SE \MmluCatFrontierPSInSE{}), against \MmluCatFrontierPS{} over all \MmluCatFrontierPSAllN{}.

On TruthfulQA the model answers \TqaAcc{} of the questions correctly under multiple-choice scoring, the $0.9$ quantile of the risk is \TqaQnine{}, and the same holds. At this level these two tasks test the guarantee (every online method reaches the target) but not the weighting, because no weighting can help when the quantity being estimated barely changes. We report them so that the reader can see the setting in which drift-awareness has nothing to offer.

\paragraph{MMLU at levels where the target quantile moves.}
The target quantile moves more at higher levels. For the category blocks the range over the four categories of the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of the risk is \MmluSpreadAOne{} at $\alpha=0.1$, \MmluSpreadATwo{} at $\alpha=0.2$ and \MmluSpreadAThree{} at $\alpha=0.3$ (the $0.7$ quantile runs from \MmluSevenLo{} to \MmluSevenHi{}), so MMLU at $\alpha=0.3$ is a second real task on which the target quantile moves. We ran the category blocks at these two further levels with every constant as declared, and wrote down before the runs that any effect of the drift-aware components should grow with the movement of the target. It does not (Table~\ref{tab:mmlu_robust}). PromptShift-CRC minus the matched baseline in post-shift violation is \MmluATwoPSvsMatchedPost{} (SE \MmluATwoPSvsMatchedPostSE{}) at $\alpha=0.2$ and \MmluAThreePSvsMatchedPost{} (SE \MmluAThreePSvsMatchedPostSE{}) at $\alpha=0.3$, and the frontier gap of PromptShift-CRC is \MmluATwoFrontierPS{} (SE \MmluATwoFrontierPSSE{}) and \MmluAThreeFrontierPS{} (SE \MmluAThreeFrontierPSSE{}), against \MmluCatFrontierPS{} at $\alpha=0.1$. At $\alpha=0.3$ the gap over the \MmluAThreeFrontierPSInN{} orderings inside the range of the window sweep is \MmluAThreeFrontierPSIn{} (SE \MmluAThreeFrontierPSInSE{}). The criterion we had fixed for claiming that the components help at a moving target (a gap of at most $-0.005$, and more than two standard errors, at $\alpha=0.3$) is not met. As on RealToxicityPrompts, the components move post-shift violation by less than half a percentage point at equal sharpness, so a moving target is not sufficient for these weights to help.

\paragraph{RealToxicityPrompts: the upper tail moves.}
We drew \RtpN{} prompts, continued each with GPT-2 (one nucleus sample of $20$ tokens), and scored the continuation with a public toxicity classifier. Only \RtpFracHigh{} of the continuations have risk above $0.5$, and they are concentrated in prompts that are themselves toxic. Split by quartiles of the prompt toxicity annotation, the $0.9$ quantile of the risk is \RtpQnineLo{} in the lowest band and \RtpQnineHi{} in the highest, so a change of band is a real shift of the target quantile. The ordering uses only the annotation of the prompt, not the model's output. In the three lowest bands the $0.9$ quantile lies below $0.06$, close to the classifier's noise floor, so the intervention rate at $\tau=0.5$ carries little information on this task and we do not interpret it.

Static calibration fails here (coverage \RtpStaticCov{}, post-shift violation \RtpStaticPost{}), and rolling calibration is better but still above target after shifts (\RtpRollingPost{}). All online methods reach the target coverage (Table~\ref{tab:rtp_bandblock}, Figure~\ref{fig:frontier-rtp}). Among them the differences are small. Post-shift violation is \RtpPSPost{} for PromptShift-CRC, \RtpMatchedPost{} for the matched baseline, \RtpAdaptWPost{} for Adaptive CRC with the window $W$ and \RtpAdaptLongPost{} with the longest window of the sweep. Paired by ordering, PromptShift-CRC minus the matched baseline is \RtpPSvsMatchedPost{} (SE \RtpPSvsMatchedPostSE{}, PromptShift-CRC lower in \RtpPSvsMatchedPostLower{} of \RtpPSvsMatchedPostN{} orderings). On this stream a longer window works about as well as PromptShift-CRC, which is the opposite of the synthetic streams, where short windows were best. The frontier gap is \RtpFrontierPS{} (SE \RtpFrontierPSSE{}; \RtpFrontierPSIn{}, SE \RtpFrontierPSInSE{}, over the \RtpFrontierPSInN{} of \RtpFrontierPSAllN{} orderings inside the range of the sweep) for PromptShift-CRC, \RtpFrontierKernelUpd{} (SE \RtpFrontierKernelUpdSE{}) for the kernel weights with the update, and \RtpFrontierDtACI{} (SE \RtpFrontierDtACISE{}) for DtACI. The kernel with the median-distance bandwidth barely localises on sentence embeddings (the effective size of the kernel rule is close to its pool length), so its behaviour is close to that of uniform weights; this is a property of the rule with that bandwidth and we did not tune it.

\subsection{Robustness to the window choice}\label{sec:res-window}

The results suggest that the value of PromptShift-CRC is that no window has to be chosen. The best window for Adaptive CRC depends on the stream. It is short on the synthetic streams and long on RealToxicityPrompts, and the window that is best in hindsight is known only afterwards. We state the comparison in two ways.

\emph{Rank.} Among the five Adaptive CRC windows and PromptShift-CRC, PromptShift-CRC has mean rank \regSynRank{} on the synthetic streams, \regMmluCatRank{} and \regMmluSubjRank{} on MMLU, \regRtpRank{} on RealToxicityPrompts and \regTqaRank{} on TruthfulQA (Table~\ref{tab:regret}; $1$ is the lowest violation and $3.5$ is the middle of six). It is in the better half on MMLU and RealToxicityPrompts, and not on the synthetic streams or on TruthfulQA. In every setting it is better than the worst window, by between two-fifths and two-thirds of the spread of the family.

\emph{Choosing the window elsewhere.} The best window in hindsight is selected on the data it is scored on, which biases the comparison against PromptShift-CRC. A fairer protocol chooses the window on the other families of streams and applies it to the held-out family (Table~\ref{tab:lofo}). PromptShift-CRC minus the chosen window is \lofosynthetic{} (synthetic), \lofoMMLUcategories{} and \lofoMMLUsubjects{} (MMLU), \lofoRTPbands{} (RealToxicityPrompts) and \lofoTruthfulQAcategories{} (TruthfulQA). It is lower in four of the five families (Table~\ref{tab:lofo} gives standard errors): clearly so on the synthetic streams and on RealToxicityPrompts, within two standard errors on the two MMLU families, and it loses on TruthfulQA (\lofoTruthfulQAcategories{}, SE \lofoTruthfulQAcategoriesSE{}). The best window changes from the shortest (synthetic) to the longest (RealToxicityPrompts), and that change is the argument for a rule that does not choose a window. We do not claim that PromptShift-CRC beats a well-chosen window; the evidence is that it avoids a poorly chosen one.

\subsection{What each component does}\label{sec:res-ablation}

On the synthetic streams and on RealToxicityPrompts the same pattern holds (Tables~\ref{tab:synthetic} and~\ref{tab:rtp_bandblock}). The online update is the component that matters most: removing it moves post-shift violation from \synPSPost{} to \synNoUpdPost{} on the synthetic streams and from \RtpPSPost{} to \RtpNoUpdPost{} on RealToxicityPrompts. The recency window with the gate is next: the variant that keeps only the gate and the recency window has post-shift violation \synGateRecPost{} (synthetic) and \RtpGateRecPost{} (RealToxicityPrompts), close to PromptShift-CRC's \synPSPost{} and \RtpPSPost{}. The kernel weights contribute least. With them alone and the update, post-shift violation is \synKernelUpdPost{} on the synthetic streams, higher than PromptShift-CRC's, with a sharper threshold. These components move post-shift violation by less than one percentage point and the mean threshold by up to $1.6$ points, and the sign depends on the stream.

Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} puts every real setting with a post-shift analysis on one axis, the range over regimes of the target quantile of the risk. In the \MechRows{} settings the range runs from \MechSpreadMin{} to \MechSpreadMax{}, and the frontier gap of PromptShift-CRC is at most \MechGapMax{} in absolute value in every one of them, whether it is taken over all orderings or over those inside the range of the window sweep. The largest differences from the matched baseline are on RealToxicityPrompts, where PromptShift-CRC minus the matched baseline in post-shift violation is between \RtpAlphaHighPSvsMatchedPost{} and \RtpPSvsMatchedPost{} at the three GPT-2 levels, but a longer window does as well, so these are not gains over the frontier. So the data do not show a setting in which the drift-aware components recover more than a fraction of a percentage point of post-shift violation at equal sharpness, whatever the movement of the target quantile.

\subsection{Does the computed drift predict violations?}\label{sec:res-trace}

An earlier draft described the post-gate drift $D_t$ as a useful diagnostic. We tested whether the pre-gate drift, which does not depend on the scores, predicts violations, with three statistics fixed before the traces were analysed (Table~\ref{tab:trace}): the violation rate in the steps of a stream whose drift is in its top decile minus the rate elsewhere, the AUC of the drift for the violation indicator, and, across the shifts of a family, the Spearman correlation between the jump in drift at a boundary and the post-shift violation. We had fixed the criterion for success as a top-decile difference of at least $0.02$ (and more than two standard errors) or an AUC of at least $0.55$, on at least one real family.

The top-decile criterion is met on one of the three real families, MMLU categories (\TraceMmluTop{}, SE \TraceMmluTopSE{}). The AUC criterion is met on none (\TraceMmluAuc{} on MMLU, \TraceRtpAuc{} and \TraceRtpQAuc{} on RealToxicityPrompts). By the rule we fixed in advance, which required either criterion on one real family, the drift therefore counts as predictive. We do not describe it as an alarm, because the sign reverses on RealToxicityPrompts, where the AUC is also below $0.5$, a case the rule did not anticipate. The result is not a consistent alarm. On RealToxicityPrompts the sign reverses: the steps with the highest drift have fewer violations than the others (\TraceRtpTop{} with GPT-2 and \TraceRtpQTop{} with Qwen2.5-0.5B). On all three real families the shift-level correlation is negative (\TraceRealRhoLo{} to \TraceRealRhoHi{}), so the larger the jump in drift at a boundary, the lower the violation rate after it. On the synthetic streams the step-level association is positive (\TraceSynTopLo{} to \TraceSynTopHi{}), but the shift-level correlation changes sign from stream to stream. The matched baseline gives the same signs. On the real streams the pre-gate drift is almost flat across a boundary (on RealToxicityPrompts \TraceRtpDBefore{} in the 20 steps before and \TraceRtpDAfter{} in the 25 steps after), and the violation rate in the first 25 steps after a boundary is \TraceRtpErrFirst{} against \TraceRtpErrLater{} in the next 125 steps on RealToxicityPrompts, with no excess on MMLU (\TraceMmluErrFirst{} and \TraceMmluErrLater{}). We therefore describe the drift as a measure of how unlike its pool a prompt is, and not as an alarm for validity.

\subsection{A published localised competitor: L-ARC}\label{sec:res-larc}

Localised adaptive risk control (L-ARC, \citealp{zecchin2024localized}) is the published method closest to PromptShift-CRC: an online threshold that depends on the features through a kernel. We ran it on every stream of the main tables (Table~\ref{tab:larc}). The update and its constants are those of the authors; the features are the same unit-norm embeddings as for every other method, with no rescaling, and the threshold starts from the calibration quantile, as it does for quantile tracking. Before the runs we fixed the authors' defaults (step size $\eta_1=1$, kernel length scale $1$) as the primary configuration and decided to report three other configurations in full (a step size of $0.1$, and a length scale matched to our bandwidth; Table~\ref{tab:larc_all}). We claim no coverage guarantee for L-ARC in this setting.

With its defaults L-ARC holds the target on the synthetic streams (coverage \LarcSynCovLo{} to \LarcSynCovHi{}) but lies above the Adaptive CRC frontier by \LarcSynLo{} to \LarcSynHi{} in post-shift violation on all five streams, where PromptShift-CRC is within \PSfrontierMaxAbs{}. With the smaller step it does not reach the target (coverage \LarcSynSmallCovLo{} to \LarcSynSmallCovHi{}) and lies above the frontier by \LarcSynSmallLo{} to \LarcSynSmallHi{}. On MMLU and TruthfulQA it is on the frontier, as every method is (gaps \LarcMmluCatGap{} for category blocks, \LarcMmluSubjGap{} for subject blocks and \LarcTqaGap{} for TruthfulQA). On RealToxicityPrompts it is below the frontier with the GPT-2 continuations (\LarcRtpGap{}, SE \LarcRtpGapSE{}; \LarcRtpGapIn{} over the \LarcRtpGapInN{} orderings inside the range of the sweep) and above it with the Qwen2.5-0.5B continuations (\LarcRtpQGap{}, SE \LarcRtpQGapSE{}), where the gaps of PromptShift-CRC are \RtpFrontierPS{} and \RtpQFrontierPS{}. L-ARC minus PromptShift-CRC in post-shift violation is \LarcRtpMinusPS{} (SE \LarcRtpMinusPSSE{}) and \LarcRtpQMinusPS{} (SE \LarcRtpQMinusPSSE{}) on the two runs, and in mean threshold \LarcRtpThr{} and \LarcRtpQThr{}.

The two sentences we had written in advance for this comparison do not fit the outcome, because on the GPT-2 run both L-ARC and PromptShift-CRC are at or below the frontier. We therefore state it directly. With its authors' defaults L-ARC is not better than PromptShift-CRC against the frontier in a consistent way: it is well above the frontier on the synthetic streams, on it on MMLU and TruthfulQA, below it on one RealToxicityPrompts run and above it on the other.

\subsection{Do the weights help when the kernel localises?}\label{sec:res-bw}

The kernel at the median bandwidth is close to uniform (Section~\ref{sec:res-real}), and every block ordering above visits each regime once, so a past prompt like the current one is rarely in the pool when it would matter. We tested both limits with two designs fixed in advance (Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth}). First, we repeated the band-block and category-block runs with the kernel bandwidth set to $h/4$, $h/2$, $h$ and $2h$, with the gate centre and slope rescaled with it, and report every setting. Second, we built orderings in which blocks of $128$ prompts cycle through the regimes (seven visits to each toxicity band, four to each MMLU category), so that the previous visit to a regime is still in the pool of $768$ steps, and ran the same four bandwidths on them: on RealToxicityPrompts with both generators and on MMLU at $\alpha=0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.3$.

The sharper kernels do localise. On RealToxicityPrompts with GPT-2 the effective size of the kernel-plus-update rule falls from \BwKUNeffOneRtp{} at $h$ to \BwKUNeffQuarterRtp{} at $h/4$, and on MMLU from \BwKUNeffOneMmlu{} to \BwKUNeffQuarterMmlu{}; the effective size of PromptShift-CRC falls from \BwPSNeffOne{} to \BwPSNeffQuarter{}, because its gate then falls back on the recent window. In the block orderings the frontier gap of PromptShift-CRC is between \BwPSSweepLo{} and \BwPSSweepHi{} across the four bandwidths and three settings. In the recurring-regime orderings it is within \BwPSRecurMaxGap{} of zero in every setting at every bandwidth, and PromptShift-CRC is within \BwPSRecurMaxMatched{} of the matched baseline.

The numerical criterion we had fixed for a positive finding (a frontier gap below $-0.005$ and more than two standard errors, for a kernel variant at a bandwidth) can be read over all $24$ orderings or over those inside the range of the window sweep, and the text we wrote in advance did not say which. Over all orderings it is met in \BwPass{} of the \BwCells{} cells. Both are the same rule, kernel weights with the update (the variant without the recency fallback coincides with it), at $h/4$ on the GPT-2 block orderings (\BwPassGap{}, SE \BwPassSE{}). In none of those $24$ orderings does the mean threshold lie inside the range of the sweep: it is \BwPassThr{}, against \BwShortThr{} for the shortest window and \BwMatchedThr{} for the matched baseline. The rule is much blunter than any window, so its gap is measured against the end of the curve and is not a comparison at equal sharpness. It does not reappear with the Qwen2.5-0.5B continuations (\BwKUQuarterQwenGap{}, SE \BwKUQuarterQwenSE{}), and on MMLU the same variant has the opposite sign (\BwKUQuarterMmluGap{}, SE \BwKUQuarterMmluSE{}).

Over the orderings inside the range, in the cells that have at least five of them, the criterion is met in \BwInsidePass{} of \BwInsideCells{} cells. They are the same variant, the no-gate rule (a fixed half-and-half mix of the kernel weights and the recent window) at $h/4$, on the two RealToxicityPrompts runs: \BwLeadGptTwoIn{} (SE \BwLeadGptTwoInSE{}) over \BwLeadGptTwoInN{} of $24$ orderings with GPT-2, and \BwLeadQwenIn{} (SE \BwLeadQwenInSE{}) over \BwLeadQwenInN{} of $24$ with Qwen2.5-0.5B. Over all $24$ orderings the gaps are \BwLeadGptTwoAll{} (SE \BwLeadGptTwoAllSE{}) and \BwLeadQwenAll{} (SE \BwLeadQwenAllSE{}). This is the only configuration in the study that lies about half a percentage point below the frontier, and we regard it as a lead to test, not as a finding; this is a judgment made after the result, because the rule we fixed in advance had no correction for the \BwInsideCells{} comparisons. It is two of \BwInsideCells{} comparisons, and at a threshold of two standard errors about three cells would be expected by chance if no variant helped, although the cells are correlated. The two runs use the same prompts and orderings. It does not appear on MMLU or in the recurring-regime orderings. It was not the rule we proposed. In the other cells with at least five orderings inside the range, the gap is smaller than half a percentage point in absolute value.

DtACI, which has no kernel, lies clearly below the frontier on the recurring-regime orderings of RealToxicityPrompts, where its gap is \BwDtaciRecurGptTwo{} (SE \BwDtaciRecurGptTwoSE{}) with GPT-2 and \BwDtaciRecurQwen{} (SE \BwDtaciRecurQwenSE{}) with Qwen2.5-0.5B, with all $24$ orderings inside the range of the sweep.
