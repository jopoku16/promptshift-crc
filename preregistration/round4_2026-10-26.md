Runs: category-block, subject-block, no-shift and the other families as in the main MMLU run, both risks, all 67 orderings, `run_real_streams.py mmlu --alpha 0.2` and `--alpha 0.3` (without `--only`).
Prediction: the effect of the drift-aware components is ordered alpha = 0.3 <= 0.2 <= 0.1 (more negative where the quantile moves more).
Primary statistic: frontier gap of PromptShift-CRC on the category-block family (24 orderings), mean and standard error over orderings, reported for all 24 units and for the units whose mean threshold lies inside the range of the window sweep.
Secondary: PromptShift-CRC minus the matched baseline in post-shift violation (mean, SE, number lower); frontier gaps of DtACI and of kernel-plus-update; the component contrasts of Table 2; intervention rate at tau = 0.5.

## B. Does the weighting help when the kernel localises?
B1, bandwidth sweep: h' in {h/4, h/2, h, 2h}, gate centre 1.1h', slope 2.5/h', on RealToxicityPrompts band blocks (GPT-2 and Qwen2.5-0.5B, alpha = 0.1) and MMLU category blocks (alpha = 0.1). The main results keep h. The sweep is reported whole, in one table. No bandwidth is selected.
B2, recurring-regime orderings: blocks of length R = 128 cycle through a permutation of the regimes until a regime is exhausted (RealToxicityPrompts: 4 bands x 7 blocks = 28 blocks, 3584 items; MMLU: 4 categories x 4 blocks = 16 blocks, 2048 items), the items of a regime are taken in a fixed random order (seed from zlib.crc32 of the stream key), 24 permutations of the regime order, post-shift window L = R = 128 after every block boundary past the prefix. The period (4R = 512) is shorter than the pool (3W = 768), so the previous visit to a regime is inside the pool. Runs: RealToxicityPrompts both generators at alpha = 0.1; MMLU at alpha = 0.1 and at the levels of item A. Adaptive CRC with the same window sweep is run on the same streams.
Statistic: frontier gap and PromptShift-CRC minus matched, mean and SE over orderings, with inside-range counts, for every kernel variant at every bandwidth.
Kill criterion: if, over B1 and B2, no kernel variant has a frontier gap more negative than -0.005 with |mean| > 2 SE, the paper says: "With a kernel four times sharper than the median rule, and with orderings that return to a past regime while it is still in the pool, the kernel weights move post-shift violation by less than half a percentage point at equal sharpness; on sentence embeddings of these prompts the upper tail of the risk is not a function the kernel can find." If a variant passes, the paper says: "Weighting helps when regimes recur and the kernel is sharp ([h']): [X] points of post-shift violation at equal sharpness; with the block orderings and the median bandwidth it does not."

## C. Does the computed drift predict violations?
Streams: RealToxicityPrompts band blocks (both generators), MMLU category blocks (alpha = 0.1), and the five synthetic streams (30 seeds). Methods: PromptShift-CRC and the matched baseline. Per step after the prefix, record D_t^pre (pre-gate drift, a function of features and pool only), D_t, n_eff,t and err_t.
Statistics, per stream then averaged over orderings (seeds for synthetic) with SE: (i) violation rate in the steps whose D_t^pre is in the top decile of that stream minus the rate in the other steps; (ii) AUC of D_t^pre for err_t; (iii) across the shifts of a family, the Spearman correlation between the jump in D_t^pre at the boundary (mean over the 20 steps after minus mean over the 20 steps before) and the post-shift violation (secondary, reported without a threshold).
"Works" means (i) >= 0.02 with |mean| > 2 SE, or (ii) AUC >= 0.55, on at least one real family. If it does not work, the paper says: "The post-gate drift does not flag violations at the step level: after a boundary the online update absorbs the shift within a few dozen steps while D_t stays high for the whole block; D_t is a measure of novelty, not of threshold error, and we no longer describe it as a diagnostic of validity."

## Amendment 2: item E, L-ARC (written 2026-10-26 after the L-ARC code and module were read and tested on synthetic data, before any L-ARC run on the paper's streams)
Implementation: `scripts/baselines_larc.py` (update equations 17 and 18 of Zecchin and Simeone 2024; notes in `scripts/L_ARC_NOTES.md`). Constants: kappa = 1, lambda = 1e-4, step eta_t = eta_1 / sqrt(k) with k = 1 at the first step after the calibration prefix, f_1 = 0, c_1 = the (1 - alpha) quantile of the prefix risks (the warm start the quantile-tracking baseline already receives; this differs from the authors' c_1 = 0 and is stated in the paper). Configurations, all four reported in full: eta_1 in {0.1, 1} (the paper's text names 0.1 for one experiment, the authors' scripts pass 1) x kernel length scale in {1 (the authors' default), matched to the paper's own kernel exp(-d^2 / h^2)}. The primary configuration, used in the sentence of the paper, is the authors' defaults: eta_1 = 1 and length scale 1. The other three are sensitivity analyses.
Streams: the five synthetic streams (30 seeds), MMLU category and subject blocks (alpha = 0.1), RealToxicityPrompts band blocks (both generators, alpha = 0.1), TruthfulQA category blocks (alpha = 0.1), probability risk and 0/1 error as in the main tables. The frontier curve is the Adaptive CRC window sweep already on disk for each stream.
Statistics: frontier gap (post-shift violation minus the curve at the method's own mean threshold; all units and inside-range units), the paired difference L-ARC minus PromptShift-CRC in post-shift violation and in mean threshold, and the rank of L-ARC among the window family.
